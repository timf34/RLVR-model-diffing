- **1000 datasets per scenario.** Rates are proportions ± Monte Carlo standard error.

## Notes

- **Primary test: calibrated.** No scenario shows a raw false-positive rate more than 2 MC SE above 0.05. It is
  conservative when rates are moderate (about 0.03-0.04) because cells hold only 2 samples per task (the
  permutation distribution is discrete), and almost never rejects in sparse data (0.000-0.001). Holm family-wise
  error is at most 0.055 ± 0.007 (within-cell correlation scenario) and 0.03 elsewhere.
- **Within-cell correlation (`d_cluster`).** Treating prompt-level variation as random (logit sd 1, shared by a
  cell's 2 samples) raises the sample-level tests to the nominal level (primary 0.046 / 0.051; secondary E 0.051).
  Stronger prompt-level variation would push them above 0.05. The tests are exact for "these prompts" (samples
  are independent draws given the prompt); this is stated in the ANALYSIS.md header. The task-level sign-flip p
  is robust to it (0.016), but with 6 tasks its smallest value is 0.031 > 0.025, so it can never pass Holm alone.
- **Composite null (`e_nuisance`).** Equal exception effects in both models plus model level differences do not
  inflate the primary test (0.026 / 0.034): the permutation reference (no exception effect in either model) is
  wider than the true null spread, i.e. conservative.
- **Intervals.** The two-stage bootstrap interval covers the true contrast 95-97% of the time with moderate rates
  and at the planted effects. As a test, "interval excludes 0 in either presentation" occurs 8-10% under the null
  (≈ two 5% chances), uncorrected, so the interval is not a substitute for the Holm-corrected p.
- **Sparse data and equivalence.** In sparse data the raw equivalence rule would call the models "similar" 3-4% of
  the time from degenerate intervals (e.g. [0, 0]). The report's guards prevent this: a contrast whose models are
  both low-rate (≤ 2/12 at every wording; flagged in 92-94% of sparse datasets) is "hard to estimate", and an
  interval of zero width is "degenerate", never "similar". "Similar" was never wrongly claimed at the planted
  +0.20 or +0.30 effects.
- **Power is low.** At base rate 0.20 (12 rewarded completions per cell), a true +0.30 primary contrast is
  rejected after Holm in about 24% of datasets per presentation (both presentations 9%); +0.20 in about 10%.
  Near a zero base rate, +0.30 is rejected about 60% of the time. A non-significant primary result is not evidence
  of no difference: read the interval, and use the equivalence rule before calling models similar.
- **Secondary E tests** (uncorrected, 16 per dataset): mean per-test false-positive rate 0.000-0.051 across
  scenarios; with 16 uncorrected tests, about one nominal "hit" in every 1-2 datasets is expected by chance.
